CLINICAL GUIDELINES: MENTAL HEALTH — BASIC GUIDE
World Health Organization mhGAP Guidelines - 2024

SECTION 1: DEPRESSION

Depression is a common mental health condition characterized by persistent low mood and loss of interest in activities. It affects how a person feels, thinks, and handles daily activities.

Symptoms (must persist for at least 2 weeks):
- Persistent sadness or feeling empty
- Loss of interest or pleasure in activities previously enjoyed
- Changes in appetite (eating too much or too little)
- Sleep disturbances (insomnia or excessive sleeping)
- Fatigue and low energy
- Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt
- Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
- Withdrawal from social activities
- Physical symptoms without clear cause (headaches, stomach pain)
- In severe cases: thoughts of death or suicide

Treatment:
- Mild depression: regular physical exercise, structured daily activities, psychological support
- Moderate to severe: antidepressant medication (SSRIs like Fluoxetine are first-line) combined with psychological therapy (CBT, interpersonal therapy)
- Duration of medication: minimum 9-12 months after symptoms improve
- Do NOT stop medication abruptly — gradual tapering required

When to refer urgently:
- Active suicidal thoughts or plans
- Self-harm behavior
- Psychotic symptoms (hallucinations, delusions)
- Not eating or drinking for extended period

SECTION 2: ANXIETY DISORDERS

Anxiety is excessive worry or fear that interferes with daily functioning. It becomes a disorder when it is persistent, uncontrollable, and causes significant distress.

Types:
- Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD): constant worry about many things
- Panic Disorder: sudden intense episodes of fear (panic attacks)
- Social Anxiety: intense fear of social situations
- Phobias: intense fear of specific objects or situations

Symptoms of anxiety:
- Excessive worrying
- Restlessness, feeling on edge
- Difficulty concentrating
- Irritability
- Muscle tension
- Sleep problems
- Rapid heartbeat, sweating, trembling (panic attacks)

Treatment:
- Relaxation techniques (deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation)
- Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) — most effective psychological treatment
- Regular physical exercise (30 minutes, 5 times per week)
- Limiting caffeine and alcohol
- Medication: SSRIs (Sertraline, Escitalopram) for moderate to severe cases
- Benzodiazepines: only short-term (2-4 weeks) due to dependence risk

SECTION 3: STRESS MANAGEMENT

Stress is the body's response to demands or challenges. While short-term stress can be helpful, chronic stress damages physical and mental health.

Effects of chronic stress:
- Weakened immune system
- High blood pressure
- Digestive problems
- Muscle tension and pain
- Sleep disorders
- Anxiety and depression
- Difficulty concentrating

Evidence-based stress management:
- Regular physical exercise (natural stress reliever)
- Adequate sleep (7-9 hours for adults)
- Mindfulness meditation (even 10 minutes daily helps)
- Maintaining social connections
- Time management and setting realistic goals
- Limiting screen time before bed
- Avoiding excessive use of alcohol or substances as coping
- Seeking professional help when stress becomes unmanageable

SECTION 4: SLEEP DISORDERS

Insomnia is the most common sleep disorder, characterized by difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early.

Sleep hygiene recommendations:
- Maintain consistent sleep and wake times (even on weekends)
- Keep bedroom dark, quiet, and cool (18-22°C ideal)
- Avoid screens 1 hour before bed (blue light suppresses melatonin)
- Limit caffeine after 2 PM
- Regular exercise, but not within 3 hours of bedtime
- Avoid large meals close to bedtime
- Use bed only for sleep (not work or scrolling)
- If unable to sleep after 20 minutes, get up and do something relaxing, then return

When to seek medical help:
- Insomnia lasting more than 3 months
- Excessive daytime sleepiness affecting work/safety
- Loud snoring with breathing pauses (sleep apnea)
- Unusual behaviors during sleep (sleepwalking, acting out dreams)
