CLINICAL GUIDELINES: COMMON DISEASES AND CONDITIONS
World Health Organization - Integrated Management Reference

SECTION 1: COMMON COLD AND INFLUENZA

The common cold is a viral upper respiratory tract infection caused mainly by rhinoviruses. Influenza (flu) is caused by influenza viruses and is more severe.

Symptoms of common cold:
- Runny or stuffy nose
- Sneezing
- Sore throat
- Mild cough
- Low-grade fever (usually below 38.5°C)
- Body aches (mild)
- Duration: 7-10 days

Symptoms of influenza (flu):
- High fever (39-40°C), sudden onset
- Severe body aches and muscle pain
- Headache
- Dry cough
- Extreme fatigue
- Chills and sweats
- Duration: 1-2 weeks

Treatment:
- Rest and hydration
- Paracetamol for fever and pain
- Antiviral medications (Oseltamivir/Tamiflu) if started within 48 hours for flu
- Antibiotics are NOT effective against cold or flu (they are viral)
- Cough suppressants and decongestants for symptom relief

Prevention:
- Annual flu vaccination
- Hand hygiene
- Respiratory etiquette (cover coughs and sneezes)
- Avoid touching face with unwashed hands

SECTION 2: COUGH

Cough is a reflex that helps clear the airways. Types:
- Dry cough: no mucus production (viral infection, allergies, asthma)
- Wet/productive cough: produces mucus/phlegm (bacterial infection, bronchitis, pneumonia)
- Chronic cough: lasting more than 8 weeks (requires medical evaluation)

When to worry about cough:
- Coughing up blood (hemoptysis)
- Cough lasting more than 3 weeks
- High fever with cough
- Difficulty breathing
- Unexplained weight loss with chronic cough (possible TB)

Treatment:
- Warm fluids (honey and lemon for soothing, honey NOT for children under 1 year)
- Cough suppressants (dextromethorphan) for dry cough
- Expectorants (guaifenesin) for productive cough
- Inhaled bronchodilators if asthma-related
- Antibiotics only if bacterial infection confirmed

SECTION 3: HEADACHE

Types of headache:
- Tension headache: dull, pressing pain on both sides (most common)
- Migraine: severe, throbbing, usually one-sided, with nausea and light sensitivity
- Cluster headache: severe pain around one eye, recurring in clusters
- Secondary headache: caused by underlying condition (sinusitis, high blood pressure, medication overuse)

Danger signs (seek immediate medical attention):
- Sudden, severe headache ("worst headache of my life") — possible stroke
- Headache with fever and stiff neck — possible meningitis
- Headache after head injury
- Headache with vision changes
- New headache in person over 50

Treatment:
- Paracetamol 500-1000mg or Ibuprofen 200-400mg
- Rest in a quiet, dark room (for migraine)
- Adequate hydration
- Stress management and regular sleep
- Avoid triggers (certain foods, lack of sleep, screen time)

SECTION 4: STOMACH PAIN AND DIGESTIVE ISSUES

Common causes of stomach pain:
- Gastritis (stomach lining inflammation)
- Acid reflux / GERD (burning sensation in chest)
- Food poisoning (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea within hours of eating)
- Appendicitis (sharp pain in lower right abdomen — EMERGENCY)
- Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS) — chronic, recurring pain with altered bowel habits
- Constipation

Diarrhea management:
- Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) — most important treatment
- Continue eating — small, bland meals
- Zinc supplementation for children (20mg/day for 10-14 days)
- Antibiotics only for specific bacterial causes (bloody diarrhea, cholera)
- Seek medical attention if: blood in stool, severe dehydration, fever >38.5°C, lasting >3 days

SECTION 5: SKIN CONDITIONS

Common skin problems:
- Eczema (atopic dermatitis): dry, itchy, inflamed skin. Treatment: moisturizers, topical steroids
- Fungal infections (ringworm, athlete's foot): circular rash, itching. Treatment: antifungal creams
- Acne: blocked pores causing pimples. Treatment: benzoyl peroxide, retinoids, keep skin clean
- Urticaria (hives): raised, itchy welts. Usually allergic reaction. Treatment: antihistamines
- Wound care: clean with water, apply antiseptic, cover with sterile bandage, watch for infection signs (redness spreading, pus, fever)

SECTION 6: BACK PAIN

Most common cause: muscle strain or sprain from poor posture, heavy lifting, or sudden movements.

Management:
- Stay active — bed rest for more than 1-2 days worsens outcomes
- Pain relief: Paracetamol, Ibuprofen, or muscle relaxants short-term
- Heat or cold packs on affected area
- Gentle stretching and strengthening exercises
- Maintain good posture
- Seek medical attention if: pain radiates down leg (sciatica), numbness/tingling, loss of bladder/bowel control, pain after trauma
