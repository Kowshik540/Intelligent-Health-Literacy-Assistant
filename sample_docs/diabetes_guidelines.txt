CLINICAL GUIDELINES: TYPE 2 DIABETES MELLITUS
World Health Organization - 2024 Update

SECTION 1: DEFINITION AND DIAGNOSIS

Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM) is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by hyperglycemia resulting from insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency. It accounts for approximately 90-95% of all diabetes cases worldwide.

Diagnostic Criteria:
- Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG) >= 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L)
- 2-hour Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT) >= 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L)
- HbA1c >= 6.5% (48 mmol/mol)
- Random plasma glucose >= 200 mg/dL with classic symptoms

SECTION 2: RISK FACTORS

Major risk factors for Type 2 Diabetes include:
1. Obesity (BMI >= 30 kg/m2) - strongest modifiable risk factor
2. Physical inactivity - less than 150 minutes/week of moderate activity
3. Family history - first-degree relative with diabetes (2-6x increased risk)
4. Age >= 45 years
5. Hypertension (blood pressure >= 140/90 mmHg)
6. Dyslipidemia (HDL < 35 mg/dL or triglycerides > 250 mg/dL)
7. History of gestational diabetes
8. Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)

SECTION 3: SYMPTOMS

Common symptoms of uncontrolled diabetes:
- Polyuria (frequent urination) - due to osmotic diuresis
- Polydipsia (excessive thirst) - compensatory response to fluid loss
- Polyphagia (increased hunger) - due to cellular glucose deprivation
- Unexplained weight loss - despite increased food intake
- Fatigue and weakness
- Blurred vision - from lens swelling due to hyperglycemia
- Slow wound healing
- Recurrent infections (skin, urinary tract)

SECTION 4: TREATMENT GUIDELINES

First-Line Treatment:
Metformin is the recommended first-line pharmacological therapy for T2DM.
- Starting dose: 500 mg once daily with meals
- Target dose: 1000 mg twice daily
- Mechanism: Reduces hepatic glucose production, improves insulin sensitivity
- Contraindications: eGFR < 30 mL/min, active liver disease

Second-Line Options (when Metformin alone is insufficient):
- SGLT2 inhibitors (e.g., Empagliflozin) - cardiovascular and renal benefits
- GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g., Liraglutide) - weight loss benefit
- DPP-4 inhibitors (e.g., Sitagliptin) - weight neutral
- Sulfonylureas (e.g., Glimepiride) - low cost but hypoglycemia risk

SECTION 5: MONITORING

Recommended monitoring schedule:
- HbA1c: Every 3 months until target achieved, then every 6 months
- Target HbA1c: < 7.0% for most adults, individualized for elderly
- Blood pressure: Every visit, target < 130/80 mmHg
- Lipid panel: Annually
- Kidney function (eGFR, urine albumin): Annually
- Eye examination: Annually (diabetic retinopathy screening)
- Foot examination: Every visit (neuropathy and vascular assessment)

SECTION 6: COMPLICATIONS

Microvascular complications:
- Diabetic retinopathy - leading cause of blindness in working-age adults
- Diabetic nephropathy - leading cause of end-stage renal disease
- Diabetic neuropathy - affects up to 50% of patients

Macrovascular complications:
- Coronary artery disease - 2-4x increased risk
- Cerebrovascular disease (stroke) - 2x increased risk
- Peripheral arterial disease - 4x increased risk

SECTION 7: LIFESTYLE MODIFICATIONS

Evidence-based lifestyle recommendations:
- Weight loss: 5-7% body weight reduction significantly improves glycemic control
- Physical activity: 150 minutes/week moderate-intensity aerobic exercise
- Diet: Mediterranean diet or DASH diet recommended
- Smoking cessation: Reduces cardiovascular risk by 50% within 1 year
- Alcohol: Moderate consumption only (1 drink/day women, 2 drinks/day men)
- Sleep: 7-9 hours per night; treat sleep apnea if present
