CLINICAL GUIDELINES: FEVER MANAGEMENT
World Health Organization - 2024

SECTION 1: DEFINITION

Fever is defined as a body temperature above 38°C (100.4°F). It is a common symptom that indicates the body is fighting an infection or other condition. Normal body temperature is approximately 36.1°C to 37.2°C (97°F to 99°F).

Classification by temperature:
- Low-grade fever: 38.0°C – 38.9°C (100.4°F – 102°F)
- Moderate fever: 39.0°C – 39.9°C (102.2°F – 103.8°F)
- High fever: 40.0°C or above (104°F or above)
- Hyperpyrexia: 41.5°C or above (106.7°F) — medical emergency

SECTION 2: COMMON CAUSES

Fever is most commonly caused by:
- Viral infections (influenza, common cold, COVID-19, dengue)
- Bacterial infections (pneumonia, urinary tract infection, strep throat)
- Inflammatory conditions (rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease)
- Heat exhaustion or heat stroke
- Certain medications (antibiotics, seizure medications)
- Immunizations (temporary low-grade fever is normal after vaccination)
- Teething in infants (mild fever only)

SECTION 3: SYMPTOMS ASSOCIATED WITH FEVER

When you have fever, you may also experience:
- Chills and shivering
- Sweating
- Headache
- Muscle aches and body pain
- Loss of appetite
- Dehydration
- Fatigue and weakness
- Irritability (especially in children)

SECTION 4: WHEN TO SEEK MEDICAL ATTENTION

Seek immediate medical care if:
- Temperature exceeds 40°C (104°F) in adults
- Fever lasts more than 3 days without improvement
- Fever is accompanied by severe headache, stiff neck, or confusion
- Difficulty breathing or chest pain
- Persistent vomiting or diarrhea
- Skin rash appears with fever
- Seizures occur (febrile seizures)
- In infants under 3 months: any fever above 38°C requires immediate evaluation

SECTION 5: TREATMENT

Non-pharmacological measures:
- Rest and adequate sleep
- Stay hydrated (water, oral rehydration solutions, clear broths)
- Wear light clothing
- Keep room temperature comfortable
- Use lukewarm sponge baths (avoid cold water)
- Do not use ice baths or alcohol rubs

Pharmacological treatment:
- Paracetamol (Acetaminophen): First-line treatment
  Adults: 500mg–1000mg every 4-6 hours (max 4g/day)
  Children: 10-15mg/kg every 4-6 hours
- Ibuprofen: Alternative or adjunct
  Adults: 200-400mg every 6-8 hours
  Children: 5-10mg/kg every 6-8 hours
- Aspirin: NOT recommended for children under 16 (risk of Reye's syndrome)

SECTION 6: PREVENTION

- Maintain good hand hygiene (wash hands frequently)
- Get recommended vaccinations
- Avoid close contact with sick individuals
- Maintain a healthy immune system through proper nutrition and sleep
- Practice food safety (proper cooking, storage)
