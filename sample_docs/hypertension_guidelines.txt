CLINICAL GUIDELINES: HYPERTENSION MANAGEMENT
American Heart Association / American College of Cardiology - 2024

SECTION 1: DEFINITION AND CLASSIFICATION

Hypertension (High Blood Pressure) is defined as persistently elevated arterial blood pressure. It is the leading modifiable risk factor for cardiovascular disease worldwide.

Blood Pressure Categories:
- Normal: Systolic < 120 mmHg AND Diastolic < 80 mmHg
- Elevated: Systolic 120-129 mmHg AND Diastolic < 80 mmHg
- Stage 1 Hypertension: Systolic 130-139 mmHg OR Diastolic 80-89 mmHg
- Stage 2 Hypertension: Systolic >= 140 mmHg OR Diastolic >= 90 mmHg
- Hypertensive Crisis: Systolic > 180 mmHg AND/OR Diastolic > 120 mmHg

SECTION 2: RISK FACTORS

Non-modifiable risk factors:
- Age (risk increases with age)
- Family history of hypertension
- Race/ethnicity (higher prevalence in African Americans)
- Gender (men at higher risk before age 55; women after menopause)

Modifiable risk factors:
- Excessive sodium intake (> 2300 mg/day)
- Obesity (BMI >= 30)
- Physical inactivity
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Smoking
- Chronic stress
- Sleep apnea

SECTION 3: SYMPTOMS

Hypertension is often called the "silent killer" because most patients are asymptomatic. Symptoms typically only appear when blood pressure reaches dangerously high levels:
- Severe headaches
- Nosebleeds
- Shortness of breath
- Dizziness
- Chest pain
- Visual changes
- Blood in urine

SECTION 4: DIAGNOSIS

Blood pressure measurement guidelines:
- Use validated automatic device
- Patient should be seated, relaxed for 5 minutes
- Arm supported at heart level
- Take 2-3 readings, 1 minute apart, average the results
- Confirm with out-of-office measurements (home BP or ambulatory monitoring)
- Diagnosis requires elevated readings on 2 or more occasions

SECTION 5: TREATMENT

Non-pharmacological interventions (all patients):
- DASH diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension)
- Sodium restriction to < 1500 mg/day (optimal) or < 2300 mg/day
- Regular aerobic exercise: 90-150 minutes/week
- Weight loss if overweight: each 1 kg lost reduces BP by ~1 mmHg
- Limit alcohol: <= 2 drinks/day men, <= 1 drink/day women
- Potassium supplementation: 3500-5000 mg/day from dietary sources

First-line pharmacological treatment:
- ACE inhibitors (e.g., Lisinopril, Enalapril)
- ARBs (e.g., Losartan, Valsartan)
- Calcium channel blockers (e.g., Amlodipine, Nifedipine)
- Thiazide diuretics (e.g., Hydrochlorothiazide, Chlorthalidone)

Treatment targets:
- General population: < 130/80 mmHg
- Elderly (>= 65 years): < 130 systolic mmHg if tolerated
- Diabetes patients: < 130/80 mmHg
- Chronic kidney disease: < 130/80 mmHg

SECTION 6: COMPLICATIONS OF UNCONTROLLED HYPERTENSION

Target organ damage:
- Heart: Left ventricular hypertrophy, heart failure, coronary artery disease
- Brain: Stroke (ischemic and hemorrhagic), vascular dementia
- Kidneys: Chronic kidney disease, renal failure
- Eyes: Hypertensive retinopathy, vision loss
- Blood vessels: Aortic aneurysm, peripheral arterial disease

SECTION 7: MONITORING

Follow-up schedule:
- Monthly until BP at target
- Then every 3-6 months once stable
- Annual assessment: kidney function, electrolytes, lipids
- Home BP monitoring recommended for all hypertensive patients
