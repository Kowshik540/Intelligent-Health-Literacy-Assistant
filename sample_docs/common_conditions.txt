CLINICAL GUIDELINES: COMMON MEDICAL CONDITIONS
World Health Organization - Integrated Management Reference 2024

SECTION 1: HEADACHE

Types of headache:
- Tension headache: Most common. Dull, aching pain on both sides. Caused by stress, poor posture, lack of sleep.
- Migraine: Severe throbbing pain, usually one side. May include nausea, sensitivity to light and sound. Lasts 4-72 hours.
- Cluster headache: Severe pain around one eye. Occurs in clusters over weeks or months.
- Sinus headache: Pain and pressure around forehead, cheeks, and eyes. Associated with sinus congestion.

Treatment for tension headache:
- Paracetamol 500-1000mg or Ibuprofen 200-400mg
- Adequate hydration and rest
- Stress management techniques
- Regular sleep schedule

When to seek emergency care for headache:
- Sudden, severe headache ("worst headache of life") — may indicate subarachnoid hemorrhage
- Headache with fever and stiff neck — may indicate meningitis
- Headache after head injury
- Headache with vision changes, confusion, or weakness

SECTION 2: COMMON COLD AND INFLUENZA

Common Cold:
- Caused by rhinoviruses (over 200 types)
- Symptoms: runny nose, sneezing, sore throat, mild cough, low-grade fever
- Duration: 7-10 days
- Treatment: Rest, fluids, paracetamol for discomfort. No antibiotics needed.

Influenza (Flu):
- Caused by influenza A or B viruses
- Symptoms: high fever (38-40°C), severe body aches, fatigue, dry cough, headache
- Duration: 1-2 weeks
- Treatment: Rest, fluids, antivirals (Oseltamivir) if started within 48 hours of symptoms
- Prevention: Annual influenza vaccination recommended

Key difference: Flu symptoms are more severe and sudden compared to common cold.

SECTION 3: COUGH

Types of cough:
- Acute cough (< 3 weeks): Usually caused by viral upper respiratory infections
- Subacute cough (3-8 weeks): Often post-infectious
- Chronic cough (> 8 weeks): May indicate asthma, GERD, or chronic bronchitis

Treatment:
- Acute viral cough: Honey (for adults and children > 1 year), warm fluids, steam inhalation
- Do NOT give cough suppressants to children under 6 years
- Seek medical care if: cough lasts > 3 weeks, blood in sputum, difficulty breathing, weight loss

SECTION 4: DIARRHEA

Definition: Three or more loose or watery stools per day.

Causes:
- Viral (rotavirus, norovirus) — most common
- Bacterial (E. coli, Salmonella, Cholera)
- Parasitic (Giardia, Amoeba)
- Food intolerance or medication side effects

Treatment:
- Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS): Most important treatment to prevent dehydration
  Recipe: 1 liter clean water + 6 teaspoons sugar + 1/2 teaspoon salt
- Continue feeding (do not fast)
- Zinc supplementation for children (20mg/day for 10-14 days)
- Antibiotics only for specific bacterial causes (prescribed by doctor)

Danger signs requiring immediate care:
- Blood in stool
- Signs of severe dehydration (dry mouth, no tears, sunken eyes, no urination for 6+ hours)
- Persistent vomiting preventing oral rehydration
- Fever above 39°C with diarrhea

SECTION 5: CHEST PAIN

Not all chest pain is a heart attack. Common causes:
- Musculoskeletal: Sharp, localized pain that worsens with movement or breathing. Often due to muscle strain.
- Gastrointestinal (GERD): Burning sensation, worse after eating or lying down.
- Anxiety/Panic attack: Chest tightness with rapid heartbeat, sweating, and fear. Usually resolves within 20 minutes.
- Pulmonary: Sharp pain with breathing (pleurisy), may indicate pneumonia or blood clot.
- Cardiac: Pressure or squeezing sensation in center of chest, may radiate to arm, jaw, or back.

IMMEDIATELY CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES (112) if chest pain:
- Feels like pressure or squeezing
- Lasts more than 15 minutes
- Spreads to arm, jaw, neck, or back
- Is accompanied by shortness of breath, sweating, nausea
- Occurs with dizziness or fainting

SECTION 6: BACK PAIN

Most common type: Non-specific lower back pain (mechanical)
- Affects 80% of people at some point in life
- Usually resolves within 6 weeks

Causes:
- Poor posture and prolonged sitting
- Muscle strain from lifting heavy objects incorrectly
- Age-related wear (disc degeneration)
- Lack of exercise and weak core muscles

Treatment:
- Stay active — bed rest is NOT recommended
- Over-the-counter pain relief (paracetamol, ibuprofen)
- Apply heat or ice to affected area
- Gentle stretching and walking
- Maintain good posture
- Physiotherapy for persistent pain

Red flags (seek immediate care):
- Back pain with loss of bladder or bowel control
- Numbness or weakness in legs
- Back pain after significant trauma
- Unexplained weight loss with back pain
- Pain that worsens at night or does not improve with rest

SECTION 7: ALLERGIES

Common types:
- Allergic rhinitis (hay fever): Sneezing, runny nose, itchy eyes. Triggered by pollen, dust, pet dander.
- Food allergies: Common triggers — nuts, shellfish, eggs, milk, wheat.
- Drug allergies: Most common — penicillin, NSAIDs, sulfa drugs.
- Contact dermatitis: Skin reaction to metals, latex, cosmetics, plants.

Treatment:
- Antihistamines (cetirizine, loratadine) for mild symptoms
- Nasal corticosteroids for allergic rhinitis
- Avoidance of known triggers
- Epinephrine (EpiPen) for severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis)

Signs of anaphylaxis (EMERGENCY — call 112):
- Difficulty breathing or wheezing
- Swelling of throat, tongue, or face
- Rapid drop in blood pressure
- Dizziness or loss of consciousness
- Widespread hives with breathing difficulty

SECTION 8: SKIN CONDITIONS

Eczema (Atopic Dermatitis):
- Chronic itchy, red, dry skin patches
- Common in children, often improves with age
- Treatment: Moisturizers, topical corticosteroids, avoid irritants

Acne:
- Blocked hair follicles with oil and dead skin
- Common in teenagers due to hormonal changes
- Treatment: Gentle cleansing, benzoyl peroxide, retinoids for severe cases

Fungal infections (Ringworm, Athlete's foot):
- Circular red patches (ringworm) or itchy cracked skin between toes (athlete's foot)
- Treatment: Topical antifungal cream (clotrimazole, miconazole) for 2-4 weeks
- Keep affected area clean and dry

SECTION 9: MENTAL HEALTH BASICS

Common conditions:
- Anxiety: Excessive worry, restlessness, difficulty concentrating, sleep problems
- Depression: Persistent sadness, loss of interest, fatigue, changes in appetite/sleep
- Stress: Physical and emotional tension from demanding situations

When to seek help:
- Symptoms persist for more than 2 weeks
- Interfering with daily life, work, or relationships
- Thoughts of self-harm or suicide (immediate help needed)

Self-care:
- Regular physical exercise (30 minutes most days)
- Adequate sleep (7-9 hours)
- Social connection with friends and family
- Limiting alcohol and avoiding substance use
- Mindfulness or relaxation techniques

SECTION 10: PREGNANCY BASICS

Important prenatal care:
- Start folic acid (400mcg daily) before conception and during first 12 weeks
- Regular antenatal check-ups as recommended by healthcare provider
- Balanced diet with iron and calcium-rich foods
- Avoid alcohol, smoking, and unnecessary medications
- Stay physically active (moderate exercise is safe for most pregnancies)

Warning signs during pregnancy (seek immediate care):
- Vaginal bleeding
- Severe abdominal pain
- Severe headache with vision changes
- Sudden swelling of face or hands
- Reduced or absent fetal movement after 28 weeks
- Leaking fluid from vagina before term
