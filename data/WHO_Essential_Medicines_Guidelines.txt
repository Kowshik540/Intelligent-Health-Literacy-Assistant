SECTION 1: FEVER AND HIGH TEMPERATURE

Definition: Fever is a body temperature above 38°C (100.4°F). It is a symptom, not a disease, indicating the immune system is responding to infection.

Treatment of Fever:
First-line treatment: Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) 500-1000mg every 4-6 hours for adults. Maximum dose: 4g per day. For children: 10-15mg/kg every 4-6 hours.
Alternative: Ibuprofen 200-400mg every 6-8 hours for adults. For children: 5-10mg/kg every 6-8 hours.
Supportive care: Rest, adequate fluid intake (2-3 litres daily), light clothing, lukewarm sponging.
Do NOT use aspirin in children under 16 years due to risk of Reye's syndrome.

When to seek emergency care for fever:
- Temperature above 40°C (104°F) in adults
- Any fever in infants under 3 months
- Fever lasting more than 3 days without improvement
- Fever with stiff neck, severe headache, or confusion
- Fever with difficulty breathing or chest pain
- Fever with seizures (febrile convulsions)
- Fever with rash that does not blanch under pressure

SECTION 2: HEADACHE

Types of Headache:
1. Tension headache: Dull, pressing pain on both sides of head. Most common type. Caused by stress, muscle tension, poor posture, dehydration, or lack of sleep.
2. Migraine: Throbbing pain usually on one side. May include nausea, vomiting, sensitivity to light and sound. Can last 4-72 hours. Triggers include certain foods, hormonal changes, stress, and sleep disturbances.
3. Cluster headache: Severe pain around one eye. Occurs in clusters over weeks or months.
4. Secondary headache: Caused by underlying conditions (sinusitis, high blood pressure, infection, head injury).

Treatment of Headache:
First-line: Paracetamol 500-1000mg or Ibuprofen 200-400mg.
For migraine: Sumatriptan 50-100mg at onset. Rest in quiet, dark room.
Supportive care: Adequate hydration (drink water), stress management, regular sleep schedule, avoid known triggers.
Prevention: Regular exercise, consistent sleep routine, stress reduction techniques.

When to seek emergency care for headache:
- Sudden severe headache (thunderclap headache) — may indicate stroke or brain hemorrhage
- Headache with fever, stiff neck, and confusion — may indicate meningitis
- Headache after head injury
- Headache with vision changes or eye pain
- New headache in person over 50 years old
- Headache that wakes you from sleep

SECTION 3: CHEST PAIN

Chest pain requires careful assessment as it may indicate life-threatening conditions.

Causes of Chest Pain:
Cardiac: Heart attack (myocardial infarction), angina, pericarditis
Respiratory: Pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, pleurisy, pneumothorax
Gastrointestinal: Acid reflux (GERD), oesophageal spasm
Musculoskeletal: Costochondritis, muscle strain, rib fracture

EMERGENCY — Call ambulance (112/108) immediately if chest pain:
- Is crushing, squeezing, or feels like pressure on chest
- Spreads to arm, jaw, neck, or back
- Comes with shortness of breath, sweating, nausea
- Lasts more than 15 minutes
- Occurs with loss of consciousness

Treatment while waiting for emergency services:
- Sit upright in comfortable position
- Chew aspirin 300mg (if not allergic)
- Do NOT lie flat if experiencing difficulty breathing
- Loosen tight clothing

SECTION 4: COMMON COLD AND RESPIRATORY INFECTIONS

Symptoms: Runny nose, sneezing, sore throat, mild cough, low-grade fever, body aches.

Treatment of Common Cold:
There is NO cure for common cold. Treatment is supportive:
- Rest and adequate sleep
- Drink plenty of fluids (warm water, herbal tea, clear broth)
- Paracetamol or Ibuprofen for body aches and fever
- Saline nasal drops for congestion
- Honey and lemon in warm water for sore throat (adults only, not for children under 1 year)
- Steam inhalation for congestion (adults)

Antibiotics do NOT work for common cold (it is caused by viruses, not bacteria).

When to see a doctor:
- Symptoms lasting more than 10 days
- High fever above 39°C
- Difficulty breathing or wheezing
- Severe sore throat with difficulty swallowing
- Ear pain or discharge
- Symptoms improving then suddenly worsening

SECTION 5: STOMACH PAIN AND DIGESTIVE ISSUES

Common causes of stomach pain:
- Gastritis: Inflammation of stomach lining. Burning pain in upper abdomen.
- Acid reflux (GERD): Burning sensation rising from stomach to throat.
- Food poisoning: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea within hours of eating contaminated food.
- Appendicitis: Sharp pain starting around navel, moving to lower right abdomen. EMERGENCY.
- Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS): Chronic recurring pain with altered bowel habits.

Treatment:
For gastritis/acid reflux: Antacids, avoid spicy/fatty food, eat smaller meals, avoid lying down after eating.
For food poisoning: Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS), clear fluids, bland diet. Zinc supplementation for children with diarrhoea.
For general stomach pain: Paracetamol (avoid NSAIDs as they can worsen gastric issues).

EMERGENCY signs — seek immediate care:
- Severe abdominal pain that does not improve
- Blood in stool or vomit
- Signs of dehydration (no urination, dry mouth, dizziness)
- Fever above 38.5°C with abdominal pain
- Abdominal pain with inability to pass gas or stool

SECTION 6: DIABETES MELLITUS

Definition: Diabetes is a chronic condition where the body cannot properly regulate blood sugar (glucose) levels.

Types:
- Type 1: Body does not produce insulin. Usually diagnosed in children/young adults. Requires insulin injections.
- Type 2: Body does not use insulin effectively. Most common (90% of cases). Often related to lifestyle factors.

Symptoms of Diabetes:
- Excessive thirst (polydipsia)
- Frequent urination (polyuria)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Blurred vision
- Fatigue and tiredness
- Slow healing of wounds
- Frequent infections
- Numbness or tingling in hands/feet

Management of Type 2 Diabetes:
Lifestyle: Regular physical activity (150 minutes/week), healthy diet, weight management, smoking cessation.
First-line medication: Metformin 500mg, gradually increasing to 2000mg/day.
Monitoring: Regular blood glucose testing, HbA1c every 3 months (target below 7%).
Complications to watch for: Eye problems (retinopathy), kidney disease (nephropathy), nerve damage (neuropathy), foot ulcers.

SECTION 7: HYPERTENSION (HIGH BLOOD PRESSURE)

Definition: Blood pressure consistently above 140/90 mmHg. Often called "silent killer" because it usually has no symptoms until complications occur.

Risk Factors: Obesity, high salt diet, physical inactivity, smoking, excessive alcohol, family history, age, stress.

Treatment:
Lifestyle modifications (first step for all patients):
- Reduce salt intake to less than 5g/day
- Regular exercise (30 minutes, 5 days/week)
- Maintain healthy weight (BMI 18.5-24.9)
- Limit alcohol
- Stop smoking
- DASH diet (fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy)

Medications (when lifestyle alone is insufficient):
- First-line: ACE inhibitors (Enalapril, Lisinopril) or Calcium channel blockers (Amlodipine)
- Target: Blood pressure below 140/90 mmHg (below 130/80 for diabetics)

EMERGENCY — Hypertensive crisis (BP above 180/120 mmHg):
Seek immediate medical care. Symptoms may include severe headache, chest pain, vision changes, confusion.

SECTION 8: MENTAL HEALTH

Depression:
Symptoms lasting more than 2 weeks: Persistent sadness, loss of interest, fatigue, sleep disturbances, appetite changes, difficulty concentrating, feelings of worthlessness, thoughts of self-harm.
Treatment: Counselling/psychotherapy, SSRIs (Fluoxetine 20mg/day as first-line), regular exercise, social support.

Anxiety:
Symptoms: Excessive worry, restlessness, muscle tension, sleep problems, difficulty concentrating, irritability.
Treatment: Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT), relaxation techniques, regular exercise. Medication if severe: SSRIs or short-term benzodiazepines.

CRISIS RESOURCES:
- India Emergency: 112
- India Ambulance: 108
- iCall Mental Health Helpline (India): 9152987821
- Vandrevala Foundation 24/7 (India): 1860-2662-345
- US Emergency: 911
- Suicide Prevention (US): 988

SECTION 9: MATERNAL HEALTH

Danger signs during pregnancy — seek immediate care:
- Vaginal bleeding at any stage
- Severe headache with blurred vision (pre-eclampsia)
- High fever
- Severe abdominal pain
- Reduced or absent fetal movement
- Swelling of face or hands
- Convulsions

Routine antenatal care recommendations (WHO):
- Minimum 8 antenatal contacts during pregnancy
- Iron and folic acid supplementation daily
- Tetanus vaccination
- Blood pressure monitoring at every visit
- Urine testing for protein and glucose
- Ultrasound before 24 weeks for dating

SECTION 10: INFECTIOUS DISEASES

Malaria:
Symptoms: High fever with chills, sweating, headache, body aches, nausea. Fever pattern may be cyclical.
Prevention: Insecticide-treated bed nets, indoor residual spraying, antimalarial prophylaxis in endemic areas.
Treatment: Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy (ACT) as first-line. Seek treatment within 24 hours of fever onset in malaria-endemic areas.

Tuberculosis (TB):
Symptoms: Persistent cough for more than 2 weeks, coughing blood, night sweats, weight loss, fever, fatigue.
Diagnosis: Sputum test, chest X-ray, GeneXpert test.
Treatment: Standard 6-month regimen (2 months RHZE + 4 months RH). Must complete full course even if feeling better.

Dengue:
Symptoms: High fever, severe headache, pain behind eyes, joint and muscle pain, rash, mild bleeding (nose/gums).
Treatment: No specific antiviral. Supportive care with Paracetamol (NOT aspirin or ibuprofen), fluid replacement, rest.
Warning signs requiring hospitalisation: Severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding, lethargy, fluid accumulation.
