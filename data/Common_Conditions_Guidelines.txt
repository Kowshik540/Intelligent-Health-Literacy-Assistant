CLINICAL GUIDELINES: COMMON MEDICAL CONDITIONS
Source: World Health Organization - Integrated Management Guidelines

SECTION 1: FEVER
Fever is a body temperature above 38 degrees C (100.4 degrees F). It is a symptom, not a disease, and usually indicates the body is responding to an infection.
General care for fever in adults: rest, drink plenty of fluids (water, oral rehydration solution, clear broths), wear light clothing, and keep the room comfortable. Paracetamol (acetaminophen) may be used to reduce fever and discomfort at standard doses on the package. Do not give aspirin to children or teenagers.
Seek medical care for fever if: temperature is above 40 degrees C (104 degrees F) in adults, fever lasts more than 3 days without improvement, fever comes with a severe headache and stiff neck, difficulty breathing, chest pain, a new rash, persistent vomiting, confusion, or seizures. In infants under 3 months, any fever needs urgent medical assessment.

SECTION 2: HEADACHE
Headache is very common and is often caused by tension, dehydration, lack of sleep, eye strain, or stress. Migraine causes a throbbing, often one-sided pain that may come with nausea and sensitivity to light.
General care for headache: rest in a quiet, dark room, drink water, manage stress, keep regular sleep, and use simple pain relief such as paracetamol or ibuprofen at standard doses if appropriate.
Seek urgent medical care if a headache is sudden and very severe, follows a head injury, comes with fever and a stiff neck, causes vision changes or weakness, or is the worst headache you have ever had.

SECTION 3: HIGH BLOOD PRESSURE (HYPERTENSION)
Blood pressure is written as systolic over diastolic (for example 120/80 mmHg). A normal reading for most adults is around 120/80 mmHg or a little below. A reading of 110/70 mmHg is within the healthy range and is generally not a concern on its own.
Blood pressure is generally considered high (hypertension) when readings are consistently at or above 140/90 mmHg. High blood pressure often has no symptoms, which is why regular checks matter. It raises the risk of stroke, heart attack, and kidney problems over time.
Lifestyle steps that help: reduce salt, eat more fruit and vegetables, stay physically active, maintain a healthy weight, limit alcohol, and avoid tobacco. Anyone with consistently high readings should see a healthcare professional.

SECTION 4: LOW BLOOD PRESSURE
Low blood pressure (hypotension) is sometimes defined as a reading below 90/60 mmHg. Many healthy people have naturally low blood pressure with no symptoms. It can occasionally cause dizziness or fainting, especially when standing up quickly. If low blood pressure causes symptoms, drink enough fluids, stand up slowly, and consult a healthcare professional.

SECTION 5: DIABETES
Diabetes is a long-term condition where blood glucose (blood sugar) stays higher than normal because the body cannot make or properly use insulin. Common symptoms can include increased thirst, frequent urination, tiredness, and unexplained weight loss, though some people have no symptoms.
Managing diabetes involves a healthy balanced diet, regular physical activity, maintaining a healthy weight, and taking any medication as prescribed. Uncontrolled diabetes can affect the eyes, kidneys, nerves, and heart over time. Regular check-ups and blood sugar monitoring are important.

SECTION 6: COUGH AND COLD
Most coughs and colds are caused by viruses and get better on their own within one to two weeks. Helpful care includes rest, fluids, and warm drinks. Antibiotics do not help viral infections.
Seek medical care if a cough lasts more than three weeks, brings up blood, comes with difficulty breathing or chest pain, or is accompanied by a high fever that does not improve.

SECTION 7: WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY HELP
Call emergency services immediately for: chest pain or pressure, sudden difficulty breathing, signs of stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech), severe bleeding, loss of consciousness, or a suspected overdose. In India dial 112 for emergencies or 108 for an ambulance.
